The book is clean; the spread is not

Sammaan is a cleaner lender that earns less. Abu Dhabi’s IHC paid ₹139 a share for control in March; the legacy clean-up cost ₹7,145cr in FY26. IHC added ₹906cr early, on 30 October; ₹2,291cr more is due by September 2027. Q2 PAT rose 28% to ₹312cr and gross NPAs are 0.15%. First-half loans of ₹10,153cr beat the ₹10,000cr plan, but costs take 62% of income, not 50%, borrowing costs 9.8% against a 9.3% goal, and ₹217cr of Q2’s ₹425cr PBT was a write-back. The shares are 12% down this year, 7% below IHC’s price and under the 200-day average (₹150), as our September desk notes flagged.

1) FY27 PAT is ₹1,180cr, 16% below the “intact” ₹1,400cr: we hold H2 at Q2’s run-rate, ₹624cr, not the ₹844cr needed; H2 disbursements must double to ₹19,947cr. 2) Write-backs fade from ₹457cr in H1 to ₹320cr in H2 and ₹250cr in FY28, then a ₹100cr charge, so PAT dips to ₹956cr before ₹1,096cr. 3) Net total income, which counts interest booked as fair-value gains, is 2.8% of assets (management: 2.9%); we take it to 3.8% by FY29 (target 3.5%) on November’s 0.25% rate rise and cheaper funding. 4) EPS is ₹9.82, ₹6.50 and ₹7.45, the last two on 1,471m shares; ROE stays under 6%.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹125 averages two methods that disagree. At 0.85x March-2028 book of ₹165 a share — today’s 0.84x diluted book, under the 0.92x IHC paid — the shares are worth ₹140. At 15x Dec-28E EPS of ₹7.21, near Aadhar Housing’s 15.2x and above the 12.6x peer median, they are worth ₹108. The gap is return on equity: 4% in FY28. We rate the shares Neutral (band: −5% to +15%); Q3 will test “intact”. Upside risks: guidance met, AAA by FY28, cheaper debt. Downside: write-backs reverse, new gold and personal loans sour, a new chairman and auditor.
