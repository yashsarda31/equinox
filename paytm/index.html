Paytm (One 97 Communications)

A full price for a fee without a start date

Paytm is at a 52-week high after two regulatory gifts. On 15 September NPCI allowed a merchant fee (MDR) of up to 0.4% on UPI payments above ₹2,000, and today the RBI approved in principle the return of the wallet. Management puts the MDR uplift at 2.5bp of GMV, about ₹710 cr a year against annualised H1 EBITDA of ₹946 cr. Yet the start date, moved from 15 October to “after Diwali”, is still not notified and the fee is under challenge in the Supreme Court. Q2 FY27 was strong: revenue rose 28% to ₹2,648 cr and EBITDA was ₹270 cr.

1) GMV grows 27%, 22% and 20% in FY27-29E. 2) MDR starts on 1 January 2027 at half the guided rate, then 2.5bp, all reaching EBITDA (₹921 cr in FY28E). 3) Default-loss guarantees resume with the largest lender, cutting contribution margin from 56% to 53% in Q3. 4) Indirect costs grow half as fast as contribution profit. EPS is then ₹17.3, ₹40.4 and ₹57.4 for FY27-29E (consensus ₹18.9 and ₹39.9). Without MDR, FY28E EPS is ₹26.5. Paytm also pays tax only on interest income: ₹7,792 cr of old losses shelter operating profit until FY30E. Fully taxed, FY28E EPS is ₹32.5.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹1,620 averages two methods. A 48x P/E, midway between the 59x paid today and the 37x median of PB Fintech, Pine Labs, Groww and MobiKwik, on fully-taxed forward EPS of ₹42.2, plus ₹17 of remaining tax shield, gives ₹2,040. A ten-year DCF (12% cost of equity, 5.5% terminal growth) plus cash gives ₹1,200. The price needs operating profit to grow 32% a year in FY30-36E; we assume 25% fading to 10%. MDR is ₹460 of our target. We rate the shares Underweight (ladder: −5% to +15%). Upside risks: platform fees, the wallet, buybacks. Downside: an adverse court ruling, guarantee losses.
