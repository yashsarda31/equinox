APL Apollo Tubes

The cost of funding Tata Steel’s assets

Tata Steel agreed to buy the promoter’s 26.6% stake at ₹2,100 a share. Its ₹2,301.40 open offer covers up to 26% of expanded voting capital. If oversubscribed, tenders are prorated. At 35% acceptance, the remaining shares would need to fetch ₹1,403 for a buyer at ₹1,717.20 to break even before tax and costs. The actual acceptance rate is unknown. APL Apollo receives none of those proceeds. It separately agreed to pay Tata ₹28,000cr for tubes and colour-coated steel assets. The shares fell 19.1% to ₹1,717.20 intraday.

The assets add 2.5m tonnes of annual capacity to Apollo’s 5m. Tata targets 15.5m by 2035, including 5m from contract manufacturer SG Spectra. The price is 24.8x projected FY27 asset EBITDA, implying ₹1,129cr. At an assumed 8%, a fully debt-funded purchase would cost ₹2,240cr a year in interest. That is nearly twice the assets’ projected EBITDA, before depreciation. Apollo’s own business earned ₹5,999 per tonne in Q2, up 15% year on year. We model ₹1,180cr of legacy EBITDA in H2 versus ₹989cr in H1, in line with management’s expectation of a stronger second half.

Our case assumes approvals by March-end and ₹14,000cr of equity issued at ₹1,750 in April. That adds 8cr shares. FY28 EBITDA rises to ₹3,740cr, yet EPS falls to ₹41.87 from ₹50.32. Our Dec-27 target of ₹1,805 averages 20x forward EBITDA and 32x forward EPS. The first method deducts ₹11,825cr of projected net debt and values the shares at ₹1,978. The second gives ₹1,630. We use multiples below the 24.8x asset purchase price and HDFC Securities’ pre-deal 35x P/E to reflect refinancing risk. Higher mill utilisation would lift EPS. A cheaper equity issue or slower refinancing would cut it. Tata’s investor call is scheduled for 5 p.m. IST today.
