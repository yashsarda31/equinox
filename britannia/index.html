Britannia

Volume is back; the margin is not

Britannia fell 3.9% the day after its Q2 FY27 results and is 1.6% below the pre-results close, while the NIFTY rose 5.2%. At ₹4,900 it is 21% below its January high and trades on 44x FY27E and 39x FY28E EPS, the bottom of its three-year range of 39-67x forward earnings. The quarter was the largest in its history: sales rose 16.6% to ₹5,542 cr on 13% volume growth. Profit did not keep up: EBITDA rose 7% and profit 10%. We rate the shares Neutral with a Dec-27 target of ₹5,460, 11% above the price.

The volume came off a base that fell 3% around last September’s GST change. Gross margin fell 200bp to 39.6% as palm oil (+17%), milk (+16%), laminate (+18%) and fuel (+45%) rose year on year against 3.5% of pricing. More increases, mostly grammage cuts on ₹5 and ₹10 packs, take about two months and will trim volume. EBITDA margin fell to 18.3% from 19.7%. We put the H2 margin at 18.0% (18.9% a year ago) and FY27E EPS at ₹110.78 against consensus ₹112.42, +9% on FY26 EPS of ₹101.22 excluding a ₹95 cr tax-provision reversal. FY28E assumes the margin recovers to 18.3%: EPS ₹126.44 against ₹127.78, down from ₹130.12 ninety days ago.

The target averages 53x, Britannia’s own three-year average, on EPS for the twelve months after Dec-27 (₹137.2), or ₹7,244, and a ten-year FCFE DCF at an 11% cost of equity and 6.5% terminal growth plus net cash, or ₹3,677 (₹4,696 at 10%). The legs are far apart: the multiple leg alone is +48%; the DCF needs free cash flow to grow 14.1% a year to FY37 against our 9.2%. The peer median of 36.7x gives ₹5,027; consensus is ₹5,702 (33 analysts, 23 buy). We would be wrong if pricing restores a 19% margin by FY28 with volume intact, earning back the old multiple. The main risk is the reverse: inflation outrunning pricing just as Reliance enters biscuits in Tamil Nadu.
