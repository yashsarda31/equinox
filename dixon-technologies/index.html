Dixon Technologies

Memory prices lift revenue, not profit

Dixon has risen 19% since its Q2 FY27 results on 30 October (NIFTY +12%) and at ₹13,120 trades at 94x FY26 adjusted EPS of ₹139.6 and 78x FY27 consensus. The headline looked poor: profit after minorities fell 49% to ₹344 cr, almost all of it a smaller fair-value gain on the Aditya Infotech stake (₹57 cr against ₹465 cr). Excluding it, profit rose 19% to ₹293 cr on revenue up 47% to ₹21,794 cr. Operating profit rose only 6%: the margin fell to 2.7% from 3.8% as the first mobile PLI scheme expired and dearer memory chips, passed through at cost, swelled revenue. H1 free cash flow was ₹980 cr negative.

1) Phones: management guides 33-34m units this year and 55-60m in FY28 with the Vivo joint venture; we take 50m, with Vivo at 12m in its first year against 18-20m guided. 2) Mobile and EMS margin stays near 2.6-2.8% until display and camera modules scale: FY28E EPS of ₹244 is 11% below consensus (₹274) on revenue 3% above. 3) As management expects, receivables unwind by March as incentive money arrives, so FY28E free cash flow is ₹947 cr. Our EPS is adjusted profit after minorities; Dixon’s reported EPS (₹271.59 for FY26) divides profit before minorities.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹9,100 averages two methods. The peer-median forward P/E of 40x (Kaynes, Amber, PG Electroplast, Syrma, Avalon) on twelve-month-forward EPS of ₹289, plus the Aditya stake at ₹148, gives ₹11,688. A ten-year DCF at 12%, plus cash, less minorities, gives ₹6,430 (₹7,650 at 11%). The price needs free cash flow to grow 31% a year to FY37E, against our 17%. What would prove us wrong: management’s FY28 plan, 57.5m phones and a 90 bp margin gain, is worth ₹16,408 on the peer multiple. Risk: ₹1,110 cr of incentive income for over-performance awaits government sign-off (₹577 cr owed onward to a customer).
