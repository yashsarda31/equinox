A booming order book at a full multiple

At ₹621 TD Power trades at 30x earnings for the twelve months after Dec-27 (81x trailing), 24.8% below the 52-week high of ₹826. Q2 FY27: revenue rose 86% to ₹841 crore, EBITDA 110% to ₹174 crore (20.6% margin, ex other income), PAT 106% to ₹124 crore. H1 revenue ₹1,481 crore (+80% y/y); FY27 guidance is now ~₹2,900-3,000 crore. Growth is export-led (90% of H1 inflow, ₹1,558 crore, +70% y/y); data-centre gas engines, hydro generators, anchored by the 10-year Siemens Energy build-to-print (13 August). Down 21% in three months; the ₹600 crore QIP at ₹775 (12 October, 0.05% below its floor) is 25% above the market.

1) H2 FY27E revenue ₹1,469 crore (+42% y/y), 21.2% EBITDA margin, EPS ₹7.27; FY27E EPS ₹13.80, 3% above consensus (₹13.40; two brokers; standalone as is Yahoo’s ₹7.64 anchor). 2) FY28E: +20% to ₹3,540 crore as the Siemens program ramps; EPS ₹18.16, 15% above consensus (₹15.75). 3) FY27E FCF of ₹242 crore after ₹130 crore capex (FY26: ₹6 crore); the ₹0.65 interim dividend has its record date today (18 November). Margins, and capex of ₹230-250 crore in FY28-29E for the Siemens build-out, are assumptions — the QIP use of proceeds is undisclosed.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹460 averages two methods. TD Power’s own three-year average P/E of 32x (range 21-50x), on the twelve months after Dec-27, gives ₹658. A ten-year DCF of free cash flow at a 12% cost of equity, plus ₹1,216 crore of net cash at Dec-27 (incl. the QIP proceeds), gives ₹272 — even at 11%, ₹318. The gap is the argument: at ₹621 the market pays for the Siemens deal today. What proves us wrong: margins hold above 21% with inflows sustaining ₹750-800 crore a quarter — then FY28 EPS toward ₹18 takes the shares past ₹460. Risk: a pause in US data-centre orders; receivables ₹953 crore (~117 days).
