Priced for failure ahead of Phase 3 data

Viking closed at $24.77, 41% below its July high, 29% below the $35.00 at which it sold 9.0m shares in September and under the lowest of 19 Street targets ($34; mean $95). Enterprise value is $2.46bn, 35% of the $7.0bn Pfizer paid up front for Metsera a year ago. In our model the price needs only a 24% chance that injectable VK2735 is approved; the base rate for a metabolic drug in Phase 3 is 56% and we use 65%. We rate VKTX Overweight, Dec-27 target $71. That is an expected value: Phase 3 data are due in 2027, after which we would value the stock near $96 or near zero.

Q3 net loss was $183.2m ($1.55 a share) as R&D rose 85% to $166.8m. VANQUISH-1 and -2 (about 5,500 patients, 78 weeks of weekly injection) complete in 2027. September’s maintenance study is the best new evidence: 17.8% weight loss after 21 weeks at 17.5 mg, and monthly dosing then kept 82–90% of the loss against 61% on placebo. The tablet is weaker: its 13-week Phase 2 lost 20% of treated patients to adverse events (13% on placebo), so we give it 45%. Cash of $960m faces $259m of convertible notes and, Viking says, lasts through at least end-2027. We forecast an FY27E loss of $5.80 a share (consensus $5.21) and about $120m of cash at Dec-27.

The target averages a risk-weighted NPV to 2040 at a 9.4% cost of equity ($37) and the large-pharma forward P/E of 15.2x on probability-weighted 2035E earnings ($105). The legs are far apart: the first gives the rest of the pipeline nothing, the second treats Viking as a going concern. Peak sales are the swing: we assume $8.5bn (Zepbound runs at $21.4bn a year), and at half that the average is $20, below the price. Launch needs about $2.0bn more than Viking holds; raised at today’s price, the target falls to $54. We would be wrong if 78-week weight loss trails tirzepatide’s or discontinuations climb.
