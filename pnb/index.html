Margins turn up; the price got there first

Punjab National Bank’s Q2 FY27 net profit rose 12.8% to ₹5,532 cr, and the shares have gained 29% since the results on 16 October (NIFTY: 9%) and now sit above all 18 broker targets (highest ₹145, mean ₹130). The profit growth came from provisions. Operating profit rose 4%; provisions fell to ₹97 cr from ₹643 cr, as a write-back on IL&FS loans (₹1,200-1,300 cr, per the call) paid for an extra ₹1,150 cr set aside for the new loss rules. Lending did improve: the margin rose 8 bp in the quarter to 2.58%, net interest income grew 11% and loans 15%. Treasury income fell 57% as bond yields rose; staff costs fell 7%.

1) Rates help first: over half of loans track the repo rate and repriced the day after the 25 bp rise. We take the margin from 2.59% in FY27E to 2.68% in FY28E; net interest income grows 12% and 16%. 2) Other income shrinks: written-off recoveries and treasury gains, 57% of it in FY26, are fading. 3) Expected-credit-loss rules start in April 2027: of the bank’s ₹8,000 cr estimate, ₹1,150 cr is provided; we charge the rest to reserves (₹6 a share) and lift loan provisions to 0.45% of loans in FY28E from 0.25% in H1. 4) The pay deal expires in October 2027; we add ₹2,700 cr a year. Group EPS before one-offs grows 12% in FY27E, then is flat.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹145 averages two methods that disagree. A sum of parts gives ₹170: the bank at 1.1x book (₹156), on a 12% cost of equity and a 12.5% long-run return on book, plus PNB Housing, the rural banks and other stakes at market value less 20% (₹14). PNB’s three-year average P/E of 6.0x gives ₹123; the shares trade at 7.3x. The price assumes an 11.6% long-run return. If returns hold at 14% under the new rules, the target rises to ₹165; at 11% it falls to ₹130. Risks: farm loans (8.6% bad), and lower bond yields, which would raise pension costs.
