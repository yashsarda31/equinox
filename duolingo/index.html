Users are back; the money has yet to follow

Duolingo’s bet is paying off in users. In February it gave up over $50m of bookings to cut friction for free learners; daily active users (DAUs) then grew 21%, 23% and 29% in Q1-Q3, and Q4 is guided similar to Q3. The shares rose 26% the day after Q3 results (3 November) and are 88% above April’s low, though 69% below the May-2025 peak. Money lags users: Q3 bookings rose 12%, paid subscribers 14% (28% at end-2025) and Adjusted EBITDA fell 2% to $78.7m. Bookings per DAU fall 13% this year on our estimates.

1) Q4 bookings are $391m against $382m guided, the Q1-Q3 average beat. 2) Q4-average DAUs grow 20% and 15% in 2027-28E to 94m, short of management’s 100m goal. 3) Paid subscribers grow 14% and 13%; bookings per subscriber stay flat as the top Max tier closes. 4) Adj. EBITDA margin recovers from 26.6% to 27.0% and 28.5%. Bookings then grow 12% and 14%, 2027E revenue is $1,387m (consensus $1,393m) and EPS is $2.75, $3.21 and $4.05 in 2026-28E. Stock compensation is 14% of revenue: after it, 2026E EBITDA is $152m, not $326m. Cash is $29 a share.

Our Dec-27 target of $140 averages two methods, both after stock compensation. A 28x EV/EBITDA, midway between today’s 39x and the 17x median of Netflix, Spotify, Match, Pinterest and Reddit, on 2027E EBITDA of $178m plus cash gives $129. A DCF (10% cost of equity, 3.5% terminal growth) plus cash gives $153. At today’s price a 10% return needs revenue growth of 12% a year in 2030-36; we assume 12% fading to 6%. We rate the shares Underweight (Neutral: −5% to +15%). Upside risks: faster conversion, lower app-store fees. Downside: DAU growth fading as one-off campaigns lapse.
