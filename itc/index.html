ITC has lost 23% this year after dividends. On 1 February 2026 GST on cigarettes rose from 28% to 40% and excise duty jumped, on a business that earned 82% of FY26 segment profit. The shares are up 16% from October’s low, 11% since Q2 FY27 results on 2 November. With the other businesses at peer multiples, ₹295 pays 14.6x forward cigarette earnings; eight listed tobacco peers trade at a median 13x. Even if cigarette profit regains its FY26 level by FY29E, our two methods average ₹292. Recovery is priced: Neutral.

Q2 FY27 showed two companies. Cigarette revenue net of excise fell 21% and segment profit 26% (Q1: 31%); ITC is passing the tax on in stages to limit the shift to smuggled brands, and profit rose 7% on Q1. Everything else grew. Other FMCG revenue rose 19% and profit 31%; paper profit rose 51% with Century Pulp and Paper (₹3,367 cr paid) in from 1 August; non-cigarette segment profit rose 23%. Group EBITDA still fell 16%. We assume cigarette profit rises 6-7% a quarter in H2, then 15% in FY28E and 8% in FY29E, to 93% of its FY26 level. EPS falls 18% in FY27E, then grows 14% and 9%.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹280 averages a sum of parts (₹277) and a ten-year DCF at a 12% cost of equity plus cash and the ITC Hotels stake (₹283). The parts: cigarettes ₹145 at 13x; other FMCG ₹64 at 37.5x, the Indian consumer-peer median; agri and paper ₹27 at an assumed 15x; ITC Infotech ₹8, at its Happiest Minds merger value less 20%; ITC Hotels ₹9; cash ₹24. Consensus has FY28E EPS back at the FY26 level, a year before we do, and a mean target of ₹319. A 4.2% dividend yield (FY27E ₹12.50, our assumption) limits the downside. Risks: another duty rise in the February 2027 Budget; illicit trade, which ITC puts at a third of the legal market. Upside: price rises that stick.
