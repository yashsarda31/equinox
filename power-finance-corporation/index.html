Power Finance Corporation

Loan repayments temper earnings growth

Power Finance Corporation funds India’s power generators, grids and distributors. Q2 FY27 standalone profit rose 12% to ₹4,987cr even as net interest income fell 3% to ₹5,121cr. A ₹180cr provision reversal replaced a ₹242cr charge a year earlier. Prepayments held loan growth to 2% year on year. Gross bad loans fell to 0.91% from 1.11% in June after PFC wrote off a fully provisioned ₹1,116cr exposure. Net bad loans stayed at 0.15%. Including REC, group profit fell 2% to ₹7,682cr. PFC shares have fallen 6.3% this year.

1) We model 3.5% loan growth in FY27, rising to 7% in FY28 as disbursements recover. That requires ₹27,900cr of net new loans in H2 after the book shrank ₹7,591cr in H1. 2) H1 net interest margin slipped 11 basis points to 3.51%. We expect H2 net interest income of ₹10,850cr, up 5% from H1. 3) We allow for ₹350cr of H2 credit charges after ₹736cr of H1 reversals. That puts FY27 profit at ₹19,412cr, 3.2% below FY26 and close to ICICI Direct’s ₹19,287cr. 4) A dividend of ₹18 per share would yield 5.4% at the current price.

Our ₹359 Dec-27 target implies 7.8% price upside. We apply 0.95x estimated Dec-27 book value, close to the current 0.99x, and 5.5x forward EPS of ₹63.82, in line with the FY26 earnings multiple. We weight both equally. The projected decline in return on equity from 20.7% in FY26 to 16.1% in FY28 limits the case for a premium to book. The proposed REC merger offers 88 PFC shares per 100 REC shares. RBI and the exchanges gave their no-objections in October. Final approvals and lower funding costs could lift the valuation. Faster prepayments or higher credit losses would weigh on earnings. The model retains PFC’s existing share count and REC dividend income.
