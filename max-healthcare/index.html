Growth picks up in H2; the price got there first

Max Healthcare’s second-quarter network revenue rose 18% to ₹3,055cr and operating EBITDA 18% to ₹820cr, a steady 26.8% margin. Network profit rose 10% to ₹448cr, excluding last year’s ₹149cr tax gain. Dropping institutional chemotherapy cut oncology to 21% of inpatient revenue from 26%; the rest grew 25%. Occupancy held at 77% with 757 more beds. Management expects growth to speed up from about 20% as that base laps. The shares fell 24% from 21 September to ₹815 on 23 October as the Supreme Court questioned drug mark-ups, and have since risen 34%, leaving them 4% up this year.

1) FY27 revenue is ₹11,990cr (+19%), with a second half of ₹6,100cr, up 21%, against ₹11,975cr at PL. 2) About 1,050 beds open by Dec-27, 19% on today’s 5,518. Brownfield beds have broken even within a quarter; the Gurugram and Mohali greenfields cost ₹70-85cr in start-up losses. 3) The margin is 26.2% in FY27, then 26.5% and 26.9%; EBITDA compounds at 19% to ₹4,472cr in FY29, level with PL. 4) EPS is ₹17.41, ₹21.91 and ₹26.98, 6% below PL in FY28 on a 22% tax rate against its 20%. Net debt stays under 1x EBITDA despite ₹2,000cr of capex a year.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹1,225 averages two methods that agree. At 28x Dec-28E EBITDA of ₹4,287cr — two turns below today’s 30x one-year-forward multiple — less ₹1,900cr of net debt, the shares are worth ₹1,214. At 48x Dec-28E EPS of ₹25.71, against 52x today, they are worth ₹1,234. The discount prices the drug-pricing case: medicines are 15-20% of hospital revenue (Jefferies). TradingView’s 26 analysts average ₹1,114. We rate the shares Neutral (band: −5% to +15%). Upside risks: faster greenfield ramp-ups, a lenient court ruling. Downside: a mark-up cap, more CGHS de-empanelment.
