An 8% week that rates do not explain

Gold settled at $3,685.50 on 19 November, down 7.9% in the week since our last note and 31% below its 29 January record; it last settled this low in September 2025. October’s Fed minutes, out on 18 November, showed nearly all officials expect to raise rates again, and futures price a December hike. Yet rates moved little: the 10-year real yield rose 10bp, to 2.88%, and the dollar 0.9%. Our model, fitted to weekly changes over 2006-23, takes 3.8% off gold per 100bp on real yields and 1.0% per 1% on the dollar: 1.3 points of the fall.

The rest is a premium deflating; since the record, rates and the dollar explain 11 points of a 31% fall. From end-2023 to the record gold rose 157%, against 24% from rates, the dollar and an 8% a year drift. Of that excess, 44% has gone, yet gold trades 51% above the model path of $2,440, level with the 1980 peak in today’s dollars. The official bid has held: central banks bought 675t in nine months, 6% more than a year earlier, though only 120t of the third quarter’s 321t was reported and the first quarter was revised from 244t to 57t. Jewellery fell 17% and bars and coins 19% in the quarter.

We stay Neutral with a Dec-27 target of $4,150 (was $4,400), 13% above the price: the weighted value of three unchanged cases. Base (50%): two more Fed hikes, real yields ease to 2.4%, the premium holds: $4,220. Bull (25%): the Fed reverses, real yields return to 1.9%, the premium rebuilds: $4,950. Bear (25%): real yields reach 3.25% and half the premium goes: $3,150. The target fell with the price because the model takes the premium where it stands. Positioning is no floor: managed money was at the 33rd percentile of its three-year range on 10 November. In rupees the fall from the record is 24%, cushioned by May’s duty rise to 15%.
