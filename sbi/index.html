State Bank of India reported a record Q2 FY27 net profit of ₹23,038 cr on 3 November, up 14%; the shares have gained 11% since (NIFTY: 6%). The record includes ₹7,288 cr from selling 6.3% of SBI Funds Management around its IPO; a year earlier Yes Bank brought ₹4,593 cr. Before these gains, and our estimate of the tax on them, profit rose 3.5% and operating profit fell 1%. Bond yields rose, turning a ₹2,877 cr trading gain into a ₹1,277 cr loss, and costs grew 10%. The lending business did well: net interest income rose 15%, loans 18%, and loan-loss provisions were 0.22% of loans.

1) Rising rates help: 49% of domestic loans are tied to the repo or T-bill rate and reprice faster than deposits, and the bank expects a 50 bp repo rise in December. We take the margin from 2.95% in FY27E to 3.02% in FY28E; net interest income grows 16% and 15%. 2) Provisions rise from a trough. Expected-credit-loss rules start on 1 April 2027: the bank estimates a ₹19,500 cr charge to reserves (₹21 a share), and we raise loan provisions to 0.42% of loans in FY28E from 0.25% in H1. 3) The wage settlement ends in October 2027; we add ₹7,800 cr a year from then. EPS before one-offs grows 9%, 16% and 8% in FY27E-29E.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹1,130 averages two methods. A sum of parts gives ₹1,145: the bank at 1.3x book (₹928), on a 12% cost of equity and a 13% long-run return on book, plus subsidiaries at market value less 20% (₹217). SBI’s three-year average P/E of 9.5x gives ₹1,123. At ₹1,080 the shares assume a 12.5% long-run return; the bank guides to 1% on assets, about 13% on equity. Consensus is ₹1,233; 34 of 40 brokers say buy. We turn positive if returns hold near 14% under the new provisioning rules (target ₹1,200). Risks: drought restructuring of up to ₹10,000 cr of farm loans; Yes Bank AT-1 ruling.
