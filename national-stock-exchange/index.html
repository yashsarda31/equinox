NSE has traded for 36 sessions and has already had its re-rating. Second-quarter revenue rose 11% to ₹4,067cr and profit 23% to ₹2,578cr, but last year’s quarter carried a ₹1,297cr SEBI settlement charge; against the first quarter, revenue fell 11% and profit 17%. Cash-market turnover grew 24%. Option premium turnover, the source of about 60% of revenue, grew 3% after August’s closing auction. The shares, sold at ₹1,785 in September, are 16% above issue and 26% above October’s ₹1,640 low.

1) FY27 revenue is ₹17,577cr (+5.9%) — first-half ₹8,627cr and a second half of ₹8,950cr, flat on a year-ago half swollen by March’s volatility. 2) EBITDA margin rises from 67.9% to 76.6%, then 77.3% and 77.6%: the settlement charge drops out and costs are largely fixed. 3) FY28 revenue grows 12% to ₹19,690cr as revised auction rules from 7 December restore option volumes. 4) EPS is ₹45.8, ₹50.5 and ₹56.2, about 4% below the ₹48.0 and ₹52.5 that brokers’ multiples imply for FY27 and FY28. The payout is 80% and the yield 1.8%; investments of ₹27,468cr are 5% of market value.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹2,065 averages two methods that agree. At 38x Dec-28E EPS of ₹54.8 — between Jefferies’ 35x and Motilal Oswal’s 40x, below BSE’s 51x trailing — the shares are worth ₹2,081. At 29x Dec-28E EBITDA of ₹16,528cr plus investments, ₹2,046. Thirteen brokers rate it Buy; their mean target of ₹2,152 is 4% away. It trades at 45x FY27E and 41x FY28E for 11% earnings growth. The franchise — 93% of cash turnover, nearly all equity futures, 68% of option premium — is not in doubt; the price is. We rate the shares Neutral (band: −5% to +15%). Upside risks: a volatility spike, a faster option rebound. Downside: new curbs or taxes on options, share loss to BSE.
