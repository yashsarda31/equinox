Jio Financial Services

Lending grows fast. Reliance still drives book value.

Jio Credit grew assets under management 154% year over year to ₹37,320cr in September. Its net interest income more than doubled to ₹302cr. Group profit still fell 51% to ₹340cr. The ₹509cr annual dividend arrived in June, while associates and joint ventures swung from a ₹217cr profit to a ₹44cr loss. Jio BlackRock managed ₹22,746cr, up 57%. Jio also distributes insurance and processes payments. The shares are down 24% year to date.

We forecast ₹48,500cr of lending assets by March, up 30% from September. That requires about ₹5,600cr of net additions per quarter, below the ₹6,653cr added in Q2. We expect ₹800cr of second-half profit on ₹3,925cr of total income as interest earnings rise. Provisions climb from ₹65cr in H1 to ₹115cr in H2 as new loans age. Gross bad loans were 0.07% of Jio Credit’s loans in September, up from 0.03% in March. Our FY28 profit estimate of ₹2,600cr is 28% below Motilal Oswal’s ₹3,630cr. We expect slower loan growth and a return on equity below 2%.

Our Neutral rating and ₹233 target imply 3.6% upside. October’s warrant conversion raised ₹5,934cr and took the share count to 685.3 crore. At 1.03x estimated December 2028 book value of ₹225, close to today’s multiple, the shares are worth ₹232. Bank of America’s proposed ₹18,268cr for up to 49.9% implies ₹18,341cr for Jio Credit before the investment. Substituting the deal value for the lender’s ₹7,379cr of book equity gives ₹233 per parent share. Both methods hold Reliance Industries (RIL) at its November close. Deal approval and faster fee growth offer upside. Higher credit losses would weigh on profit. A 15% fall in RIL shares cuts our target to ₹211, even with the lending forecast unchanged.
