Volume-led growth, full price

Nestle India fell 8% on results day (15 October) after Q2 FY27: sales rose 20% to ₹6,782 cr, led by double-digit volume growth, and profit rose 37% to ₹1,030 cr (before exceptional items). EBITDA margin was 23.8%, advertising and marketing spend rose 46% YoY, and every product group grew. H1 revenue grew 23%, FY26 15% with volume up 11%. The shares are flat on results day, 18% below the August peak, and 12m is flat while NIFTY is up 2.9%.

1) Momentum is real: NESCAFÉ has grown double-digit for 20 straight quarters, MAGGI keeps expanding rural reach (216,000 villages) and KITKAT is gaining share; GST 2.0 (Sep-2025) added a tailwind now in the base. 2) But the build is not free: A&M is up 46% YoY and stays elevated, cocoa is volatile, food CPI is rising, and management flags near-term consumption softness (Nielsen) and West Asia freight costs; new capacity lifts depreciation. Our FY27E EPS of ₹21.15 is 5% below consensus (₹22.35), which needs H2 sales up 11% and EPS up 13% Y/Y versus our 9% sales and a flat margin. EPS grows 18%, 14% and 14% in FY27E-29E.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹1,120 averages two methods. Nestle’s own 3-yr average P/E of 67.5x on earnings twelve months ahead gives ₹1,791; a ten-year DCF at a 12% cost of equity, plus net cash and the associate at book, gives ₹457. The gap is the argument: at ₹1,263 the shares need free cash flow to compound 26% a year for a decade; we project 7%. FMCG peers trade at 33.0x forward; the consensus target of ₹1,505 (36 brokers, 22 buys) is +19% above the price. What would prove us wrong: volume-led growth above 15% into FY28 with margin above 24% and FY27 EPS above ₹22. Downside: inflation, consumption slowdown, deeper de-rating of premium multiples.
