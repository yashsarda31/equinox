Full order book, stalled cash flow

HAL has fallen 6% since its Q2 FY27 results on 12 November (NIFTY: flat). Revenue rose 15% to ₹7,613 cr and profit to owners 16% to ₹1,936 cr, but less came from operations than the headline suggests. Operating EBITDA grew 12% and its margin slipped to 22.9% from 23.5%; joint-venture profit jumped to ₹97 cr from ₹7 cr; and other income, mostly interest on ₹47,451 cr of cash, was 40% of H1 pre-tax profit. Inventories rose ₹8,653 cr in six months to ₹39,504 cr and operating cash flow fell to ₹1,573 cr from ₹7,377 cr. In May management put LCA Mk1A inventory at about ₹8,100 cr, held up by engines and trials.

1) We take H2 FY27E revenue up 10%, mid-guidance, at a 31.1% operating margin against 32.1% a year ago: aircraft earn less than overhaul work, and late ones carry liquidated-damages provisions. The guided 30% full-year margin needs 32.8% in H2. Our FY27E EPS of ₹145.92 is 3% below consensus. 2) FY28E-29E revenue grows 16% and 15% as LCA Mk1A, LCH and Su-30 deliveries start against a ₹254,538 cr order book (7.7x FY26 revenue). Consensus has 22% for FY28E; we allow for past slippage. 3) EPS grows 14% and 13%; other income falls to 26% of pre-tax profit by FY29E.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹4,520 averages two methods. HAL’s own three-year average P/E of 31x on earnings twelve months ahead gives ₹5,648. A ten-year DCF of operating cash flow at 12%, plus ₹853 a share of cash, gives ₹3,400. The gap is wide: at ₹4,675 the shares need operating free cash flow to grow 19% a year to FY37E; we forecast 11%. If customer advances grow with revenue, the DCF rises to ₹4,025 and the target to ₹4,840, still Neutral. What would prove us wrong: 20 LCA Mk1A deliveries a year and margins above 30%. Risk: fixed-price work since FY24 is booked at provisional prices pending the fourth pricing review.
