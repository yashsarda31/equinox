HDFC Life has fallen 22% over twelve months (19% YTD) to ₹604, off its 27-Oct low of ₹514, at 1.98x September embedded value (₹306/share) and 52x FY28E consensus EPS, ~30% below its own 3-yr average P/E of 72x. Q2 (15-Oct): individual WRP +11%, ahead of the private industry at 10%, private share up 10bps to 16.2%, VNB +15%. All 33 brokers rate it a buy (mean target ₹719); the shares are up 10% since the print. The de-rating, not the business, is the story.

1) H1 APE +11% to ₹8,245cr; VNB ₹2,037cr grew 12%, ahead of APE, at a 24.7% margin (24.5%); retail protection grew 33% (GST base normalising after +57% last H2); ULIP 45%, non-par + annuity 34% of individual APE; sum assured +21%. 2) Our FY27E standalone EPS ₹9.92 is 5% below consensus — it needs H2 EPS up 25% Y/Y; we model +12%. FY28E ₹11.11 (+12%), FY29E ₹12.33 (+11%); VNB ₹4,500cr, IEV ₹71,000cr. 3) Solvency 180% after the ₹1,000cr preferential to HDFC Bank (Apr-26) and the ₹500cr 10-yr sub-debt approved 16-Nov. Watch: op RoEV slipped to 14.9% (15.8%), 13M persistency to 83% (86%), expense ratio to 22.3% (21.6%).

Our Dec-27 target ₹810 averages two methods. The own-3-yr-average P/E of 72x on forward-blended EPS ₹12.03 gives ₹872. An EV-anchored leg — Dec-27 IEV ₹69,500cr (Sep-26: ₹66,461cr) + PV of FY28-37 VNB (fading 11% to 6%, 12.5% RDR) + a terminal 16.3x P/VNB — gives ₹749. The gap is the argument: pay for embedded value compounding, not a cheaper multiple. At ₹604, that is +34% — Overweight. What would prove us wrong: 13M persistency below 82%, op RoEV stuck under 14%, or the IRDAI distribution-economics consultation repricing bancassurance payouts. Key risk: 58% bancassurance mix and ULIP flows.
