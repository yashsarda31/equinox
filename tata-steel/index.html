APL Apollo adds scale and a funding bill

Tata Steel fell 8.4% to ₹162.98 after it agreed to sell its tubes and colour-coated businesses to APL Apollo for up to ₹28,000cr. Tata will pay up to ₹15,511cr for the promoters’ 26.38% diluted stake and offer ₹2,301.40 a share for another 26%. Full take-up gives Tata 52.38% on a diluted basis. Subject to approvals, the combined plants would have 7.5m tonnes of annual capacity in tubes and coated steel. Management targets 15.5m by 2035, and expects the first 1m tonnes of contracted coating capacity within 24 months.

At full take-up and maximum sale proceeds, Tata and its selling subsidiary pay a net ₹4,266cr before tax and fees. With no tenders, they would receive ₹12,489cr net. APL plans to borrow for the purchase. Once consolidated, the ₹28,000cr transfer cancels within the group. We assume a March 2027 close and full debt funding. 1) FY27 group net debt reaches ₹116,461cr from ₹91,423cr in September, even after ₹1,628cr of APL net cash and ₹5,600cr of assumed H2 debt reduction. 2) FY27 EBITDA reaches ₹42,035cr, including ₹22,000cr in H2 against ₹20,035cr in H1. India earned ₹10,634cr of the group’s ₹10,665cr Q2 EBITDA. 3) FY28 adds ₹2,200cr of APL EBITDA before synergies. Higher interest keeps EPS at ₹10.91, against PL Capital’s pre-deal ₹14.50.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹163 is near the market price. At 7x Dec-28E EBITDA, in line with ICICI Securities’ pre-deal multiple, equity is worth ₹165 a share after debt and minority interests. At 13x forward EPS of ₹12.36, near PL Capital’s pre-deal FY28 multiple, it is worth ₹161. Cheaper refinancing or stronger Indian steel margins would lift earnings. A dilutive APL equity raise, weaker steel prices or fresh restrictions at IJmuiden would put the target at risk. Open-offer take-up and APL’s refinancing terms are the next tests.
