Tata Motors PV fell 8.7% today to ₹262.40, near its 52-week low, after Friday’s Q2 FY27 results cut JLR’s guidance five months after it was set. We rate the shares Neutral with a Dec-27 target of ₹275. They look cheap at 0.9x book and 6.7x consensus FY28E EPS, but our FY28E EPS of ₹18.77 is about half the consensus ₹39.03. Today’s price implies a JLR earning a 4.3% long-run EBIT margin, against 2.0% in H1 FY27 and 8.5% in FY25.

The 34% rise in Q2 revenue was measured against last year’s cyber-incident quarter. JLR’s EBIT margin fell to 1.1% from 2.8% in Q1. Its FY27 guidance, set in June at about 4% EBIT and breakeven cash flow, is now 2-3% and an outflow of about £1.25bn. Profit also leans on capitalised spending: JLR capitalised £932m of engineering in H1 against £624m of depreciation and amortisation, and the group holds ₹87,574 cr of intangible assets under development, 91% of its market value, which begin to amortise as the new electric models reach customers from early 2027. In India revenue rose 51%, yet the EBITDA margin was 4.0%, of which 2.9 points were government incentives, mostly PLI, which ends with FY28.

Our target averages two sum-of-the-parts values. On earnings multiples (JLR at 7.1x, the median forward P/E of BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Porsche and Volvo Car; Tata PV at 22.7x, the median of Maruti, M&M and Hyundai India) the shares are worth ₹243. On discounted cash flows, with JLR’s long-run EBIT margin at 5%, they are worth ₹308; each point of that margin moves the value by about ₹63. Upside risks: JLR meets its FY28 aim of double-digit EBITDA and positive cash flow, or PLI is extended. Downside risks: China, US tariffs, slow orders for the electric models, and net debt, which we see rising to ₹55,700 cr by March 2028 where management expects a peak this year.
