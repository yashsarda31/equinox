Priced for decline; contract value is growing again

Gartner closed at $178.84, 24% below its close before the 3 November results, when guidance was raised, and 30% below its 30 December high of $254.59 (YTD −29%). It trades on 10.8x FY27E consensus EPS of $16.62 and an 11.1% FY26E free-cash-flow yield; six information-services peers trade at a median 22.7x. At our 8.0% WACC the price discounts free cash flow shrinking 1.1% a year forever, yet contract value (CV) is growing again: +2.5% in Q3, from +0.8% at Dec-25. We rate IT Overweight, Dec-27 target $400.

Q3 adjusted EPS rose 30% to $3.59, but adjusted pre-tax profit rose only 3% (about 6% without Digital Markets, sold in February): a 15% smaller diluted share count and a 17% tax rate (23% a year ago) did the rest. CV of $5.34bn grew 2.5% (GTS +2.1%, GBS +3.7%) and GTS wallet retention is back to 98% from 96% at Dec-25, but the CFO said CV growth will not reach 4% by year-end. Guidance went up for a third straight quarter (FY26 adjusted EPS at least $14.65). Buybacks took $1.22bn in 9M-26 at an average $157. We assume all free cash flow buys stock at today’s price: EQe FY27E EPS is $17.49, 5% above consensus, and FY28E $20.75.

Our $400 target averages the peer-median forward P/E (22.7x FY28E EPS = $471) and a ten-year FCFF DCF after stock compensation (WACC 8.0%: rf 5.07%, beta 0.92; terminal growth 2.5%) of $325. The legs are far apart because the peers are faster-growing data franchises: at FactSet’s 14.6x the multiple gives $302; the DCF at a 9% WACC, $271. The street mean is $201 (13 brokers, 2 buys). What would prove us wrong: AI assistants replacing research seats, visible as CV growth stalling below 2% or GTS wallet retention back under 96%; a bear case (revenue −2% a year, 23% margin) is worth $149. Soft spot: GBS wallet retention (99%, from 102%).
