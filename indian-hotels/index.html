Indian Hotels

Record quarter, soft base, full price

Indian Hotels (IHCL) has risen 18% since its Q2 FY27 results on 21 October (NIFTY: 10%) and sits near a record. Revenue grew 22% to ₹2,484 cr, net profit 23% to ₹349 cr, and FY27 revenue-growth guidance went up to 15-17% from 12-14%. The quarter was strong; the comparison flatters it. A year ago rain, fewer wedding dates and rooms shut for renovation held growth back. This year Delhi hosted the BRICS summit and Taj Palace had its renovated rooms back: Delhi revenue rose 51%, Mumbai 12%. Domestic RevPAR rose 25%. Over two years revenue has grown 17% a year. Air-catering profit fell 37%.

1) In H2 FY27E hotel revenue grows 16% (guidance implies 14-18%) and catering is flat as airlines cut schedules. Our FY27E EPS of ₹15.27 is 6% below consensus, which needs H2 profit up 26% on revenue up 15%. 2) In FY28E hotels grow 12%: more wedding dates, against the BRICS quarter. Oriental Hotels merges in from 1 April 2027 (2.32 cr new shares), adding ₹560 cr of revenue. 3) Management fees pass ₹1,000 cr in FY28E; 94% of the 34,800-key pipeline is capital-light. EPS grows 18%, 17% and 14% in FY27E-29E.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹690 averages two methods. IHCL’s own three-year average P/E of 51x, on earnings twelve months ahead, gives ₹1,012. A ten-year DCF of hotel and catering cash flows at a 12% cost of equity, plus net cash, gives ₹367. The gap is the argument: at ₹890 the shares need free cash flow to grow 28% a year to FY37E; we forecast 11%. Listed hotel peers trade at 23.5x forward earnings, IHCL at 46x. The consensus target of ₹876 is also below the price, though 25 of 28 brokers say buy. What would prove us wrong: occupancy holding above 80%; fees growing faster than 20% a year. Downside: room supply in smaller cities, set to grow 12% a year.
