CP Plus (Aditya Infotech)

Record profit, but the cash has not arrived

India’s certification rule has shut out Chinese-linked cameras since April 2025, and CP Plus owner Aditya Infotech has gained most. H1 FY27 revenue rose 90% to ₹3,148 cr and pre-tax profit before a fire loss more than tripled to ₹477 cr. The shares are up 177% this year, at 71x FY27E EPS. Yet the profit has not arrived as cash: operating cash flow was −₹67 cr in H1 and −₹26 cr since April 2024, against ₹1,155 cr of pre-tax profit, as inventory climbed to ₹1,908 cr. Without September’s ₹1,500 cr placement there would be net debt of c.₹200 cr.

Q2’s 17.6% EBITDA margin is not a run-rate, as management says. Gross margin has slipped for three quarters (31.8%, 30.8%, 30.0%) as low-cost chips and memory are used up; the gain came from other expenses, 5.5% of revenue against 8.4% in Q1. At Q1’s ratio the margin was 14.7%. The 42% market share also uses a wider market than the 45.4% quoted in May (FY26 is now 34%), and underlying Q2 PAT is nearer ₹211 cr than the ₹220 cr presented, which adds back the fire loss before its tax relief. Guidance implies H2 margins of 11.8-13.8%; we sit just above, with FY27E revenue of ₹6,898 cr and EPS of ₹58.0, then ₹71.5 and ₹87.3 (consensus ₹60.0 and ₹76.7).

Our Dec-27 target of ₹2,810 averages two methods. A 49x P/E, midway between today’s 61x and the 38x median of Dixon, Kaynes, Syrma, Amber, Havells and Polycab, on forward EPS of ₹83.4 gives ₹4,090. A ten-year DCF (12% cost of equity, 5.5% terminal growth) plus cash gives ₹1,530, as working capital ties up 22% of revenue. The price needs operating profit to grow 35% a year in FY30-36E; we assume 16% fading to 8%. If gross margin holds at 30.4% the target is ₹3,100; if component prices unwind, ₹2,170. Rating: Underweight. Upside risk: recorder certification. Downside: new certified entrants, memory deflation.
