Uber closed at $63.47 on 20 November, a 52-week low, 31% below its December 2025 high of $92.57 (YTD −22%) and 9% below where it closed on the day of a Q3 beat. It trades on 14.4x FY27E consensus EPS of $4.41 and an 8.1% FY26E free-cash-flow yield. The fear is robotaxis: Waymo and Tesla running fleets that bypass Uber’s network. At our 10.0% WACC the price discounts EQe FY27E free cash flow growing 2.5% a year forever; with the operating margin frozen at this year’s 3.8% of bookings, our DCF still gives $72. We rate UBER Overweight, Dec-27 target $95.

Q3 Non-GAAP EPS rose 35% to $0.88. Gross bookings grew 20% to $59.8bn and Non-GAAP operating income 38%, a margin of 3.85% of bookings from 3.37%. Revenue grew only 10% because business-model changes cut 8 points (Mobility revenue −2%), so we forecast on bookings. Mobility earned 8.0% of its bookings, Delivery 3.7%. Q1–Q3 EPS landed on average 92% of the way up the guided range; on the same basis Q4 is $0.99. With bookings +17% and the margin +35bp a year, EQe FY27E EPS is $4.23, 4% below consensus, and FY28E $5.36. GAAP EPS ($0.25 in Q3) swings with stake revaluations.

Our $95 target averages 14.7x FY28E EPS (six-peer median) plus equity stakes at 80% of book ($5 a share), $84, and a ten-year DCF of Non-GAAP operating income less legal and restructuring costs (rf 4.92%, beta 1.33, margin capped at 5.5% of bookings, terminal growth 2.5%), $103. Uber has offered €41.50 a share for Delivery Hero ($14.8bn equity, closing H2 2027, funded with cash and debt); it is not in our forecasts beyond the 24.99% stake already held. What would prove us wrong: robotaxis taking share or price, visible as Mobility bookings growth below 10% or its margin falling; an AV bear case (growth to zero by FY32, margin frozen) is worth $43. The street mean is $98 (46 analysts).
