BSE’s September quarter was soft and its October was a record; the shares, up 6% since the 4 November results and 23% since end-September, are priced on October. Q2 FY27 revenue from operations of ₹1,433 cr rose 34% y/y but fell 9% q/q as average daily index-options premium turnover dropped to ₹22,531 cr from ₹29,615 cr in Q1; PAT was ₹809 cr (+45% y/y, −7% q/q). October premium turnover then reached ₹35,481 cr a day. We do not extrapolate it: we assume November-March reverts to ₹27,000 cr, the average of the last three reported quarters, giving FY27E EPS of ₹82.9 (+37%), 2% above consensus.

Derivatives were 70% of H1 operating revenue, so the forecast is premium turnover times tariff. 1) Volume: after +39% in FY27E we model premium growth of 12% and 10% in FY28-29E; each 10% change moves FY28E EPS by c.7%. 2) Tariff: management says derivative charges sit below market levels and will be raised in due course; we assume no increase, which leaves upside. 3) Costs: regulatory and clearing fees take c.22% of transaction charges and move with volume, holding the EBITDA margin near 67%. We forecast a 19% EPS CAGR over FY26-29E, most of it in FY27E.

That slowdown is the valuation problem: BSE trades at 46x FY27E and 41x FY28E EPS. Our Dec-27 price target of ₹3,800 applies 38x to twelve-month forward EPS at that date, the midpoint of today’s 43x and the 33x median of listed Indian market-infrastructure peers; even today’s multiple gives only ₹4,260 (+12%). A ten-year FCFE DCF (12% cost of equity, 5.5% terminal growth) gives ₹1,606, and today’s price implies c.28% annual profit growth over FY30-36. We rate the shares Neutral (ladder: −5% to +15%). Risks: curbs on weekly index options and fading volatility (downside); a tariff increase (upside).
