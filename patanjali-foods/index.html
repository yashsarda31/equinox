Revenue at a record; the spread is not

Patanjali sold a record ₹11,323cr of oil and food in the September quarter, 15.6% more than a year earlier, and earned less: net profit fell 18.8% to ₹419.8cr. Edible oils, three-quarters of revenue, grew 22.4% to ₹8,532cr on a 3.0% EBITDA margin, down from 5.2% in June as palm and soya costs outran retail prices. First-half revenue was ₹22,660cr, up 22.1%.

Three numbers drive the forecast. 1) FY27 revenue is ₹47,200cr, up 17.5% — ₹34,300cr edible oils, ₹12,900cr FMCG — against consensus ₹47,600cr. 2) EBITDA margin slips to 4.4% from 4.8%: FMCG rises to 27% of revenue but earns 8.3%, against Marico’s 19%, and cannot offset a commodity that is 73% of sales. 3) FY28 revenue grows 10% to ₹51,900cr. Profit falls 21% in FY27 on 7% higher EBITDA: FY26’s ₹1,814cr came after a negative 34% tax rate, and at 25.5% the tax line costs ₹1,060cr.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹380 averages two methods that disagree by 26%. A sum of the parts — 27x FY28E FMCG EBITDA of ₹1,416cr and 9x ₹1,184cr of edible oils, less ₹2,900cr of net debt — gives ₹423. A DCF (11.5% cost of equity, 4% terminal growth) charging ₹450cr of capex and 18% of incremental sales to working capital gives ₹335. The gap is the mix: the market pays an FMCG multiple for a balance sheet that still behaves like a crusher, with 76 working-capital days and ₹2,981cr of debt. We rate the shares Underweight (Neutral: −5% to +15%). Upside: a palm-duty cut, faster HPC ramp. Downside: another 3%-spread year.
