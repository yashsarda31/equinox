Tega’s first full quarter with Molycop shows what the acquisition bought and what it costs. Q2 FY27 revenue of ₹4,254 cr was 10.5 times a year earlier and adjusted EBITDA was ₹608 cr. Depreciation then took ₹169 cr, finance costs ₹292 cr, one-off deal costs ₹78 cr and tax ₹132 cr, leaving a loss of ₹59 cr. Net debt is ₹10,461 cr, 4.3x annualised EBITDA, including c.₹2,600 cr of redeemable preference shares. The legacy liner business grew income 18%, yet its adjusted PAT fell 65% to ₹16 cr after ₹30 cr of acquisition-loan interest.

1) Molycop: we take H2 EBITDA at ₹479 cr a quarter, midway between Q2 (₹514 cr) and the August guidance rate, then 3% volume growth and USD 18m of synergies by FY29E (USD 20m guided). 2) Legacy consumables grow 15% a year, as guided, at a 21-22% margin. 3) Tax is the swing factor: Molycop’s adjusted tax was 31% of EBIT in June-September, as holding-level interest earned almost no shield; we assume 27% and 25% in FY28-29E. Adjusted EPS is then ₹14.3, ₹38.9 and ₹66.3 for FY27-29E. Taxed at 30% of pre-tax profit instead, FY28E EPS would be ₹66.5, near the ₹69.6 consensus (range ₹43-94).

Our Dec-27 target of ₹1,680 averages two methods. Sum of the parts gives ₹1,990: legacy Tega at 16.2x forward EBITDA (peer median: AIA Engineering, Metso, Weir, FLSmidth) and Molycop at the 7.9x paid in June, less net debt and Apollo’s 15.8%. A ten-year FCFE DCF (13% cost of equity, 5.5% terminal growth) gives ₹1,365, as multiples ignore the interest and tax leak. Each turn on Molycop’s multiple is worth ₹246 a share; today’s price implies 7.6x FY28E. We rate the shares Neutral (ladder: −5% to +15%). Upside risks: refinancing the preference shares, a lower tax rate. Downside: Chinese grinding-media competition, a copper or gold downturn.
