Retail loan growth and margin recovery

HDFC Bank reported Q2 FY27 standalone net profit of ₹19,510 cr, up 5% year on year; the shares have gained 16% since the 17-Oct-26 results (NIFTY: 11%). Net interest income rose 10% to ₹34,620 cr and advances under management grew 7% year on year. Operating profit rose 6% as costs grew only 6%, while provisions were 0.13% of loans. The NIM held at 3.29% in FY27E as the loan mix shifts toward higher-yielding retail and business banking.

1) Margin stability: the cost-of-funds advantage from a 34% CASA ratio and the repo-linked loan book keep the NIM near 3.3%; we take it from 3.29% in FY27E to 3.25% in FY28E, with NII growing 9% and 11%. 2) Provisions normalise from a trough: we carry loan-loss provisions at 0.40% of loans in FY28E from 0.24% in H1 FY27, as expected-credit-loss rules begin on 1 April 2027. 3) EPS before one-offs grows 46%, 8% and 10% in FY27E-29E on our numbers, close to consensus of 53.0 and 61.3.

Our Dec-27 target of ₹1,040 averages two methods. A sum of parts gives ₹679: the bank at 1.36x Dec-27 book (₹525), on a 12% cost of equity and a 13% long-run return on book, plus subsidiaries at market value less 20% (₹154). HDFC Bank's three-year average P/E of 17.0x gives ₹1,400. At ₹846 the shares assume a 15.1% long-run return; the bank guides to about 1.8% on assets and 13-15% on equity. Consensus is ₹962; 38 of 40 brokers say buy. We turn positive if core RoE holds above 15% under the new provisioning rules (target ₹1,080). Risks: unsecured retail loan stress, a sharper-than-expected NIM decline, and any RBI action on risk weights.
